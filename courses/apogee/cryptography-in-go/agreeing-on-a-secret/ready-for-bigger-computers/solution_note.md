Only the holder of the decapsulation key can recover the 32 bytes `Encapsulate` made, so the two ends end up with one key that nobody recording the ciphertext can work out, by a method designed to hold even against a quantum computer. A ciphertext of the wrong length is refused at once, while a changed one quietly gives a different key, so the mistake shows at the first sealed message; Go's TLS uses ML-KEM-768 and X25519 together, so a recording stays closed unless both are broken.
