# the-prompt

## Briefing

Monday 25 October, at Port Ellery. On Friday night the night shift runs the passes from the Fishbowl, and Ingrid wants gctl open the whole time: one prompt that takes command after command, not a tool started again for each one. A pass lasts about ten minutes, and every restart eats into it.

## Debrief

By Monday evening gctl waits at its prompt, answers what it knows, names what it doesn't and says bye. Ingrid types `help`, reads two lines, then types `exit`. "Copy. Tomorrow it shows me Friday's passes."

# commands

## Briefing

Tuesday. gctl's prompt waits and answers, but it knows nothing yet of Friday night: five passes, five stations and the wind at Cape Wren, which stows its dish without asking anyone. Ingrid wants the passes, the stations and every stow on her screen, and a prompt that survives a typo or a broken command, because a pass doesn't wait while someone starts the tool again.

## Debrief

By Tuesday night gctl lists Friday's passes in time order, for one station or all, shows which dishes are stowed and records each new stow. Typos get an answer, and a broken command no longer takes the prompt with it. Ingrid: "Copy. Ystmark reports it slow. That's next."

# a-cache-that-knows-its-age

## Briefing

Wednesday. Over Ystmark's slow backhaul, every command waits seconds while gctl reads the night's schedule again. Keeping the passes once read would be fast, but a schedule changed at 04:00 must still reach the prompt within ten minutes, and from Thursday a refresher reloads it every minute while the night shift types. Sunniva needs it fast, and Ingrid needs it right.

## Debrief

By Thursday evening gctl answers at Ystmark in a blink, knows when its passes are ten minutes old, and stays whole while the refresher works beside it. Ask `next` at 04:00, and it says Pipit-1, in twelve minutes. Sunniva, in four words: "Fast now. Coffee holding."

# the-night-shift

## Briefing

Friday. Tonight the night shift runs every pass from your prompt, from Pipit-1 at 04:12 to Cirrine-1 at 05:47, and the forecast has Cape Wren's wind reaching 72 km/h before 05:00. They want their own settings, a record of every command for the log and the relief shift, and proof that a whole session behaves before the first pass.

## Debrief

At 04:40 Cape Wren stows, the night shift records it, and `next` moves straight on to Brine-3. The night's passes run from your prompt, and Ingrid logs one line: "gctl shell: GO for every shift." On Monday, Yusra writes: "TL;DR: customers want to book their own passes."
