# a-schedule-on-a-stick

## Briefing

The van leaves Port Ellery this week. Paz has a memory stick with the next passes, but the laptop cannot yet read it or save a report reliably. Before the last signal drops away, she needs files that open, folders that prepare themselves and a save that keeps the old schedule until the new one is complete.

## Debrief

The stick carries the day's complete schedule and a folder for reports. Failed reads and saves return reasons instead of silence. Paz sends a voice memo: "Everything's packed, even a laptop that explains itself. Next stop: thinner air!"

# everything-is-a-stream

## Briefing

At the van's first stop, Paz wants the recorder's report on the screen and in the archive at the same time. A preview must leave the rest of the recording available, and a destination that stops accepting data must say so. Before the road gets rough, she needs one reliable route from the recording to both copies.

## Debrief

The screen and archive receive the same report from one recording. Previews stop at their limit, unfinished copies report a reason, and the final lines reach the stick before the helper returns. Paz records a voice memo: "Screen and archive, word for word. I'm calling them the twins."

# line-by-line

## Briefing

The van reaches 1,200 m this week, and Paz opens the file she brought down from Salar Quieto: La Grande's controller log, 200,000 lines on where the dish pointed and how far its aim drifted. Before calibration she needs every line read, any broken record found by its number and the whole log summed up. Until then, the error she came to fix is a guess.

## Debrief

Paz sends a voice memo at the next signal: "Two hundred thousand lines, all read, and the bad one named by its line. La Grande wanders most in the afternoon heat; I'm calling her the sunflower. Next, the bookings, so calibration doesn't steal anyone's pass."

# json-in-json-out

Every booking went back to Yusra with its ID intact, and Hollis's sheet filled with short lines that still say which passes wait for confirmation. Yusra's reply opens with its summary, as always: "All bookings matched; calibration takes no customer's pass."

# hollis-spreadsheets

Week of 6 September, 2,800 m. Hollis Vey, Furrowlight's ops manager, books his passes from a spreadsheet and writes everything in capitals. His exports hold notes full of commas and line breaks, columns that move, and the odd word where a number belongs. Paz needs every sheet read exactly, so calibration never takes one of his passes, and Hollis wants a report he can open.

## Debrief

Hollis opened the van's totals in his spreadsheet: three of the five missed passes were at Salar Quieto. His reply, in capitals: SO IT IS THE DISH. Paz's voice memo: "It is the dish, Hollis. 1,040 meters to go."

# time-exactly

## Briefing

Week of 20 September, 3,200 m. The van's laptop keeps the salt flat's clock, seven hours behind UTC, while every booking and log at Port Ellery runs on UTC. Twice already a pass has landed on the wrong day in Paz's plan; on calibration day, that would mean testing La Grande during a customer's pass. Every time gctl reads or writes must name one exact moment.

## Debrief

Every time in gctl now names one exact moment, and the van's own clock shows beside UTC on every laptop. Paz's voice memo: "Hola from 3,200 meters, where the air is thin and the clocks finally agree. I've retired my seven-hour sums."

# flags-and-subcommands

## Briefing

Week of 4 October, 3,500 m. The van's laptop holds eleven scripts for passes, stations and stows, and no two agree on how to ask for help or say that something failed. Ingrid's night shift wants one command everyone can run and script against, from the van or the Fishbowl, before a misread failure costs a booked pass.

## Debrief

The team has one command now: gctl passes, stations and stow, with help that answers and failures that say why. Paz's voice memo: "The night shift ran it from the Fishbowl and got exactly what I get at 3,500 meters. I've deleted eleven scripts, well, archived them."

# files-with-the-program

## Briefing

Week of 11 October, 3,700 meters. Copying gctl onto a fresh stick, Paz left its station table behind, and the tool no longer knows where Salar Quieto is. Up here nobody can send the file again. Paz wants gctl to carry its table and report templates inside itself, and Mirela wants every test to bring its own files instead of reading the laptop's disk.

## Debrief

gctl now carries its station table and report templates inside itself, uses its defaults only when the stick has nothing better, and its tests bring their own files. Paz's memo: "Hola from 3,700 meters! I copied gctl alone onto a bare stick, and it still knows all five stations."

# parcels-from-salar-quieto

## Briefing

Week of 11 October, the last weekend at 3,700 meters. Salar Quieto's night shift sends the van two parcels from La Grande's dish controller: its settings, and its log since July. Thursday's calibration will replace La Grande's correction, so Paz wants the old one safe on the stick first. Mirela wants proof that nothing in a parcel can land outside its folder or fill the laptop.

## Debrief

Both parcels are open with no file past its cap, La Grande's settings are on the stick, every file inside its folder, and gctl writes the correction La Grande's controller reads. Mirela: "It refuses everything I sent. Good." Paz's memo: "Hola from 3,700 meters, one last time. Tomorrow, the top!"

# the-top-of-the-road

## Briefing

Week of 18 October: the van climbs the last 140 meters to Salar Quieto, 3,840 meters up. Thursday is calibration day: La Grande and the van's small dish track the same passes, and the difference becomes La Grande's correction. Not one customer's pass may be taken, and the whole day has to run from gctl and the files on Paz's stick.

## Debrief

Thursday, 23:40 UTC: La Grande points true again, and every booked pass went to its customer. Paz's last memo from the top: "Your tools ran a whole calibration day from one stick, no signal needed. The van starts home tomorrow, and the whole team wants gctl."
